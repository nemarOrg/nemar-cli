## Decided design

Settled, from the owner decisions of 2026-11-20:

- Front matter: `number: 1`, `scope: org`, `window_days: 21`, `discussion`, `opened_at` and decision fields null, shepherd the owner.
- One RFC for the whole format; `.bzx` is its single-file form.
- A section "Why the format and the package have separate names": two layers, the store and the packing of one store into one file.
  It lists the package-specific items for comment: the commenters differ (people who use electroencephalography (EEG) and magnetoencephalography (MEG) data, and people who build Zarr tools); the names `.bzx` and `biosig-zarr` (one listing ties `.bzx` to other software, and BioSig is a long-standing library); what a `.bzx` snapshot of a BIDS dataset changes around it (rate-dependent sidecar fields rewritten, `events.parquet` never packed, the light events group); and whether a fix to the packing rules raises `format_version`, with the recommendation that one `format_version` covers both.
- A section "Relation to OME-Zarr RFC-9": cited as prior art, not a dependency.
  NEMAR's packing rules are its own and do not follow later changes to RFC-9, and the RFC presents that independence as an advantage (RFC-9 was at stage R4, not accepted, when checked on 2026-11-20).
  A table compares each RFC-9 rule with NEMAR's: adopted, strengthened, changed, or not adopted.
- A section "Why not NWB on Zarr", fair and transparent in tone:
  it names `hdmf-zarr` and links `https://nwb.org/tools/core/hdmf-zarr/`, and says plainly that it is a great tool;
  then it gives the reasons it does not fit NEMAR's case: NEMAR serves human, low-frequency biosignals organized around signal files that follow BIDS principles, where a data file sits beside its metadata sidecars, not a container that holds the metadata;
  grounded in ADR 0128's three reasons (separate scope, technical incompatibility, an unsettled NWB Zarr specification), its stateless-export position, and its revisit conditions, each cited by content;
  and it is open for comment.
- **A requirement for irregular and sparse time series.**
  The Problem section states that European Data Format (EDF) cannot represent irregularly or sparsely sampled series, and that the current draft cannot either (quote the "Time axis" paragraph and open question 9 with B2's line numbers).
  The Proposal adds a normative requirement for a representation of irregular and sparse series (for example an explicit per-sample time array beside a nominal rate, or a sparse sample-and-time encoding), with the RFC choosing one candidate as the proposal and naming the alternatives.
  The questions for commenters include one on it.
  Cite the BIDS threads listed in plan section 7 by link with "checked 2026-11-20"; cite the BEP047 thread only if Q18 supplied its link, otherwise say in the RFC's change log that the citation is pending.
- **An explicit sample resolution field.**
  The Problem section quotes the root `dtype` row and the dequantization section and states what they record (the storage type and the mapping to physical values) and what they do not (the source recording's own resolution).
  The Proposal makes `dtype` explicitly the storage resolution and adds a REQUIRED root attribute for the source sample resolution: bit depth (8, 16, 24, 32 or 64) and kind (integer or floating point), with the stated relation to `dtype` and to the per-channel `scale` and `offset` (for example a 24-bit integer source in an `int16` store is quantized, and its resolution after storage is `scale`).
  The questions for commenters include one on it.
- A section "Decided, and out of scope for comment", each item citing its record: not an NWB container; one package holds one recording; entries stored without compression; written once; packages built on the researcher's machine; the license model.
- Questions for commenters: numbered, answerable, grouped (format, package, names, irregular and sparse series, resolution, why not NWB).
- "How the decision will be made": criteria written before comments open.
- A note that RFC-0001 proposes normative changes to the `2.0.0-draft.1` text, which the next draft tag would carry if accepted.

The implementer may choose: the candidate representation for irregular and sparse series (with reasons), the attribute name for the source resolution, and wording.
